# Liquid Edge Finishing Materials

> **Safety.** Aqueous latex can still carry ammonia and it **shrinks fabrics**. Read the **product** SDS. See [Liquid adhesives](/literature-reviews/liquid-adhesives-and-seam-integrity) and [Liquid ventilation](/literature-reviews/liquid-ventilation-ammonia).

## Executive summary

On liquid work, a finished rim is often textile tape or a spread coat of aqueous latex, then a combine. The wrong family shows up as shrink ripple, a stiff rim, or delamination after a heavy wet coat migrates. This page names families and risks. It does not teach how to fold a curve.

Aqueous latex adhesives **shrink fabrics**. On porous tape, the bond is mainly mechanical if adhesive enters the pores. On slick faces, polarity must match.

---

## Material families {#material-families}

| Family | Typical use | Risk cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rubberised textile tape | High-stress or curved edge | Prep stack — [Liquid latex–textile bonding](/literature-reviews/liquid-latex-textile-bonding) |
| Woven cotton binding | Absorbent mechanical key | Aqueous **shrink** |
| Smooth filament tape | Slick face | Polarity bridge |
| Heavy wet spread on tape | Industrial analog | Migration if you dry too fast — poor internal strength, delamination |

---

## Decision cues {#decision-cues}

```text
Adhesive?
  aqueous → shrink check on the textile
Surface?
  porous → mechanical key if particles enter pores
  slick → polarity match required
Coat weight?
  heavy wet coat → watch migration / delamination (paper-saturation analog)
```

A **lap** at the rim is still an overlap. Failed rims: [Liquid delamination](/literature-reviews/liquid-delamination-seam-failure). Combining definitions: [Liquid adhesives](/literature-reviews/liquid-adhesives-and-seam-integrity#wet-vs-dry).

## Deep dives (optional)

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Saturation migration</summary>

Industrial paper saturation: if solids and drying rate run away, latex migrates to the surface, internal strength drops, and the sheet can delaminate. Transfer that carefully to a heavy wet edge coat on tape. It is a paper-web analog, not a garment fold card.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Polarity and porous flow at tape edges</summary>

Non-porous tape: match polarity. Porous tape: particles must enter pores; charge must not repel. Higher filler reduces that flow. Same rules as [Liquid adhesives](/literature-reviews/liquid-adhesives-and-seam-integrity#polarity).

</details>
