# Sheet Delamination and Seam Failure

## Executive summary

Read the broken cement line before you add more glue. **Adhesive failure** leaves cement on one side only. **Cohesive failure** splits the cement layer. **Fiber-pull** vs **rubber-tear** tells you whether the cloth or the sheet was the weak link. An edge that lifts first often tracks [lap geometry](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity#lap) and stretch, not a missing extra coat.

ASTM peel methods are lab classes, not a photo atlas of fashion sheet. Use this with [Sheet adhesives](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity) and [Sheet latex–textile bonding](/literature-reviews/sheet-latex-textile-bonding).

## Peel modes {#peel-modes}

<details>
<summary>What is adhesive vs cohesive failure?</summary>

**Adhesive failure:** the cement let go from one surface. That face looks clean. **Cohesive failure:** the cement itself tore. Both sides show residue. Which mode you see tells you whether to fix prep or the glue layer.

</details>

| Mode | What you see | Likely class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adhesive | Cement on one piece only | Prep, contamination, polarity, never wetted |
| Cohesive | Cement split through its thickness | Weak or aged cement layer |
| Fiber-pull | Textile fibers stuck in the cement | Textile side lost; rubberising / construction |
| Rubber-tear | Sheet tears away from the join | Sheet weaker than the bond; check [notch](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity#notch) |

Peel **rate** can flip cohesive and adhesive in the pressure-sensitive-adhesive study cited for this series. A slow hand peel is not the same as a fast yank in wear.

## What the faces look like {#morphology}

| What you see | Likely class |
| --- | --- |
| Clean rubber, cement all on the tape or lining | Adhesive — slick nylon or no prep |
| Orange-peel texture in the dried cement | Contamination or incomplete dry |
| Edge lift only, mid-panel still stuck | Stretch / peel leverage at the lap edge |
| Rubber torn next to a sharp inner corner | Notch or design, not “more cement” |

## Decision flow {#decision-flow}

```text
1. Split the join — look at both faces
2. Classify mode (adhesive / cohesive / fiber / rubber)
3. Location? edge vs mid-panel
4. Age? fresh vs months (oxidation / heat)
5. Fix class: re-prep vs redesign the lap vs retire the piece
```

This is a sort, not a rebuild tutorial. Glue-family tree: [Sheet adhesives](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity). Rim materials: [Sheet edge finishing](/literature-reviews/sheet-edge-finishing-materials). Oils and metals: [Compatibility matrix](/literature-reviews/compatibility-matrix-metals-oils-plastics).

## Aged cement {#aged-failure}

Heat and oxidation can soften or embrittle films and adhesive layers.

If the piece has sat against copper or oily plastic, read [Sheet aging](/literature-reviews/sheet-aging-storage-and-care) and the [compatibility matrix](/literature-reviews/compatibility-matrix-metals-oils-plastics) before you recement.
