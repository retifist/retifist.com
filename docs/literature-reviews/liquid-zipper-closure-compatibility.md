# Liquid Zipper Closure Compatibility

## Executive summary

This page is what happens when **aqueous latex** (water-based) meets zipper tape. Latex adhesives shrink fabrics. They also need the latex polarity to match a non-porous tape face. Nylon and polyester coil tape are smooth filaments. Rubber does not grab them the way it grabs cotton fuzz.

Use this when you rubberise tape with liquid latex or when a water-based coat is the prep on the tape. Bonding that tape to *bought sheet* with solvent cement is the [sheet zipper](/literature-reviews/sheet-zipper-closure-compatibility) article. Glue families: [Liquid Adhesives and Seam Integrity](/literature-reviews/liquid-adhesives-and-seam-integrity). Fiber cues: [Liquid Latex Textile Bonding](/literature-reviews/liquid-latex-textile-bonding).

## Shrink and contamination {#shrink}

Handbook comparison of latex adhesives vs solvent adhesives: the water-based side shrinks fabrics, wrinkles paper, can be contaminated by some storage and application materials, and is corrosive to some metals. It is also subject to freezing.

If you wet a woven zipper tape with latex, check shrink on a scrap of the same tape before you commit the garment edge. A cotton tape that looked stable dry can pull the seam after an aqueous coat.

Metals that touch the wet adhesive belong on the [Compatibility Matrix](/literature-reviews/compatibility-matrix-metals-oils-plastics) and [Liquid Hardware & Metal Contact](/literature-reviews/liquid-hardware-metal-contact).

## Polarity on a slick tape {#polarity}

On a non-porous face, latex polarity should match the surface you are joining.

<details>
<summary>What is polarity here?</summary>

**Polarity** is how the polymer’s chemistry lines up with the other surface. The handbook’s short list puts natural rubber, SBR, BR, and IIR at the low-polarity end, chloroprene and PVC in the middle, and nitrile (NBR, XNBR) at the high end. A low-polarity NR latex on a slick nylon tape is a mismatch unless you change the face or the latex.

</details>

Porous cotton tape is a different branch: the latex has to flow into the pores, and the particle charge must not be shoved away by the fabric. See [liquid textile bonding](/literature-reviews/liquid-latex-textile-bonding).

## Nylon filament limits {#nylon}

Rayon, nylon, and polyester: continuous filament, smooth waxlike surface, poor mechanical bond unless you abrade or chemically bridge. Cotton has fiber ends that embed in rubber.

Industrial tire-cord work abrades the filament, then runs a latex–casein dip. Combined, those steps beat either one alone under dynamic conditions. That is factory lineage.

<details>
<summary>What is a prep bridge?</summary>

A **prep bridge** is abrasion, a primer, or a latex coat that gives a slick filament something to grip. “Rubberise the tape” in industrial talk is this strategy, not a home SKU.

</details>
