# Garment Selection QA for Buyers & Wearers

> **Not medical advice.** Pre-wear checks are **literacy**, not allergy testing or device certification.

## Executive summary

Before you wear or resell a latex garment, industrial dipped-goods specs offer a **borrowed vocabulary** — holes, powder, protein, odor, bloom, gauge variation — even though **fashion catsuits are not ASTM D3578 gloves**. NIOSH separates irritant, delayed chemical, and immediate protein reaction *patterns* for workplace NRL products.

Use this when buying secondhand sheet goods, inspecting a custom commission, or deciding go/no-go on first wear. See [Allergy and Skin Contact](/literature-reviews/allergy-and-skin-contact) for allergy classes — do not treat this as a patch test.

---

## Borrowed QC vocabulary {#qc-vocabulary}

ASTM **D3578** is the product standard for **dipped NR examination gloves** (holes, powder, protein, tensile). ASTM **D5151** detects water-leak holes in **medical gloves**; smallest hole size is **not** determined by the method. Fashion sheet/garments sit **outside** that scope — transfer is **analogy only**.

Dipped-goods defects include entrapped air → pinholes, webbing, low wet gel, incomplete leach → worse aging/discoloration.

<details>
<summary>What is a pinhole in dipped film?</summary>

A **pinhole** is a through-thickness void — often from entrapped air during dip or cast. Medical glove lines water-leak test for holes; at garment scale you use light and stretch inspection, not D5151 compliance.

</details>

---

## Pre-wear checklist {#pre-wear-checklist}

1. **Light + stretch** for pinholes and thin spots (dipped-film logic — not lab leak testing).
2. **Powder/talc** — medical powdered gloves were banned as unreasonable risk; garment powder can carry NRL allergens in workplace framing. Sniff and look; powder is not a protein assay.
3. **Odor** — handbook reodorants **mask** curative/putrefaction/microbial odors at 0.25–0.5 phr; root cause preferred. Sniff ≠ protein test.
4. **Bloom vs crack** — map symptoms to [Aging, Storage and Care](/literature-reviews/aging-storage-and-care).
5. **Stop rule** — new rash or breathing symptoms → stop wear; see [Allergy and Skin Contact](/literature-reviews/allergy-and-skin-contact). **Not medical advice.**

21 CFR **801.437** requires NRL caution on **medical devices** and forbids “hypoallergenic” on those devices. Retail fashion latex typically carries no equivalent labeling.

## Deep dives (optional)

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Protein assay methods (glove lineage)</summary>

ASTM **D5712** measures total aqueous extractable protein (µg/dm²) — residual content testing, not wearer diagnosis. ASTM **D6499** uses immunological measurement of **antigenic** protein; not a measure of allergenic proteins. ISO **12243** is the assay method for water-extractable protein in NR medical gloves — not sampling or safety limits. Fashion garments almost never publish these numbers.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Aging symptom inspection taxonomy</summary>

Ozone ~1–8 pphm ambient; cracks **perpendicular to stress**, widespread vs flex (local oriented) vs UV (non-oriented over exposed area). Incomplete leach leaves coagulant salts → greater discoloration under warm air, fumes, or UV. Map sticky tack (scission), hard/brittle (crosslink), chalky bloom (wax/antidegradant), oriented cracks (ozone/flex) to the aging action table; polish does not fix ozone crack morphology.

</details>
