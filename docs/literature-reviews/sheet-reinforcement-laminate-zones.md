# Sheet Reinforcement Zones

## Executive summary

Reinforcement on bought sheet is extra material at a place that will tear or peel: a disc where seams meet, a strip along a strap root, a second layer of gauge at an opening. Add it **before** final assembly traps the junction. Use this when [mapping stress zones](#zone-map) or when a junction already failed once.

Industrial fabric-lined gloves stretch less than unsupported gloves but stop a tear from running. That is a supported-glove analog, not a catsuit lining card. Glue family for sticking the extra piece down: [Sheet adhesives](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity).

---

## Zone map {#zone-map}

| Zone | Why it matters | Sheet cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seam junction (3+ panels) | Peel and tear concentrate | Disc or cross strip before close-out |
| Opening edge (neck, arm, fly) | Cyclic stretch + edge lift | Strip or doubled gauge at the tape line |
| Strap / harness root | Line load into the sheet | Wide strip; avoid a point load |
| Hardware perimeter | Stress + metal contact | Strip; copper ageing routes — [Sheet hardware](/literature-reviews/sheet-hardware-metal-contact), [Compatibility matrix](/literature-reviews/compatibility-matrix-metals-oils-plastics) |

The map is where stress concentrates, not a cut list.

## Decision cues {#decision-cues}

```text
1. What is the job?
   ├─ Tear-stop → extra sheet or a fabric liner (supported-glove analog)
   └─ Stretch management → a stiff patch in a high-bend zone will fight the body

2. Can you still reach the junction?
   ├─ Yes → add disc / strip / double gauge now
   └─ Already closed out → you are patching the outside; the trapped corner stays

3. Textile in the stack?
   ├─ Porous cotton → mechanical key possible
   └─ Smooth nylon → polarity / pretreat — [Sheet latex–textile bonding](/literature-reviews/sheet-latex-textile-bonding)
```

## Deep dives (optional)

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Supported-glove analog</summary>

Fabric-lined gloves stretch less than unsupported gloves. The liner’s job in that chapter is stopping tear propagation, not matching fashion-sheet stretch. Oil-resistance order in the same handbook neighborhood: NR &lt; CR &lt; XNBR. That is dipped-goods language, useful only as a reminder that a liner trades stretch for durability.
