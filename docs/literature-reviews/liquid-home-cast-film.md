# Home-Cast Latex Film

> **Safety.** Liquid compounds carry ammonia, coagulant salts, and metal-hygiene rules. No copper, brass, or galvanized iron on equipment that touches latex. See [Liquid ventilation and ammonia](/literature-reviews/liquid-ventilation-ammonia).

## Executive summary

This pathway forms film from liquid latex on a bed, mould, or former. Spreading, casting, and coagulant dipping each have handbook physics. Thickness comes from total solids, dwell, viscosity, and (on dips) coagulant, not from a mill stamp.

Use this when you are pouring, spreading, or dipping a film you will later cut or wear. The pathway primer is [Forming film from liquid latex](/literature-reviews/liquid-film-pathway). Geometry choices live in [Former dip, mould cast, and flat spread](/literature-reviews/liquid-former-dip-vs-mould-cast-vs-flat-spread).

---

## Lab glass spread {#lab-spread}

Handbook lab films are made by spreading compounded latex on glass with a wire-wound knife.

**Anode** order: coagulant on the glass, then latex. **Teague** order: latex first. After the film sets, leach 20–30 minutes at 40–50 °C.

That is the closest handbook locus to a flat glass bed. It is still a laboratory evaluation method.

---

## Industrial belt spreading {#belt}

US 2,415,028 describes continuous spreading of vulcanizable latex on a conveyor, including multi-ply builds. US 2,129,607 describes successive coats with drying between coats.

Those are plant-scale sheet-from-latex lineages. Blade geometry and fabric speed belong to the spread branch in the forming article.

---

## Coagulant dip for thicker walls {#coagulant}

Dry films thicker than about 8 mils usually use coagulant because the film builds faster. Wet-gauge levers on a dip are percent total solids, coagulant strength, dwell, viscosity, and withdrawal speed.

See [Coagulant dipping for wearable thickness](/literature-reviews/liquid-coagulant-dipping-wearable-thickness).

---

## Casting in a cavity {#casting}

Moulding and casting gel latex inside a cavity, mostly for hollow articles. Plaster moulds gel by water absorption plus calcium ions. Metal moulds need heat-sensitized compound. See [Heat-sensitized gelation](/literature-reviews/liquid-heat-sensitized-gelation).

---

## Humidity, air, and metals {#discipline}

Multi-coat lamination wants a humidity window around 45–50% RH. Air in the compound makes pinholes. Equipment that touches latex must stay free of copper, brass, and galvanized iron.

When a deposit fails, name the defect: [Liquid film defects](/literature-reviews/liquid-film-defect-atlas).
