# Sheet Garment Selection QA

> **Not medical advice.** These checks are literacy. They are not an allergy test and not a device certificate.

## Executive summary

Before you wear or resell a bought sheet garment, industrial glove specs give you words for holes, powder, protein, odor, and bloom. A fashion catsuit is not an ASTM D3578 exam glove. The words still help you look.

Use this when you buy secondhand sheet, inspect a custom commission, or decide go/no-go on first wear. Reaction classes: [Allergy and Skin Contact](/literature-reviews/allergy-and-skin-contact). Do not treat this page as a patch test.

---

## Borrowed QC words {#qc-vocabulary}

ASTM **D3578** is the product standard for dipped NR examination gloves: holes, powder, protein, tensile. ASTM **D5151** is a water-leak test for medical gloves. It does not report the smallest hole it can find. You are holding sheet up to a window, not running that lab method.

<details>
<summary>What is a pinhole here?</summary>

A **pinhole** is a through-thickness void. On bought sheet, look for a bright speck when you stretch a panel against light. That is inspection, not a glove leak test.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is calendered sheet?</summary>

**Calendered** sheet is factory rubber pressed to thickness between heated rollers. Fashion sheet from a supplier is usually this: it arrives ready to cut and glue, not as liquid in a bottle.

</details>

---

## Pre-wear checklist {#pre-wear-checklist}

1. **Light and stretch.** Hold each panel to a window. Stretch. Look for pinholes and thin spots.
2. **Powder.** Medical powdered gloves were banned as an unreasonable risk. Workplace NIOSH framing: powder can carry NRL allergens. If you see talc in a crotch or underarm, treat it as a soil, not as a protein number.
3. **Odor.** Factory reodorants mask curative, putrefaction, and microbial smells at **0.25–0.5 phr**. The handbook prefers fixing the cause. A sniff is not a protein assay.

   <details>
   <summary>What is phr?</summary>

   **phr** is parts per hundred rubber. 0.25–0.5 phr reodorant is a quarter to a half gram of masker per 100 grams of rubber.

   </details>

4. **Bloom vs crack.** Chalky bloom and oriented cracks are aging symptoms. Map them in [Sheet Aging, Storage and Care](/literature-reviews/sheet-aging-storage-and-care).
5. **Stop.** New rash or breathing trouble: stop wear. See [Allergy and Skin Contact](/literature-reviews/allergy-and-skin-contact).

Medical devices that contain NRL must carry a caution under 21 CFR **801.437** and must not say “hypoallergenic” on that device. Retail fashion sheet usually has no equivalent label.

Hardware stains and stiff rings around snaps: [Sheet Hardware & Metal Contact](/literature-reviews/sheet-hardware-metal-contact).

## Deep dives (optional)

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Protein assay methods (glove lineage)</summary>

ASTM **D5712** measures total aqueous extractable protein in NR products, reported in µg/dm². It is residual-protein testing, not a diagnosis of a wearer.

ASTM **D6499** measures **antigenic** protein by an immunological method. It is not a measure of allergenic proteins. The correlation is not fully established.

ISO **12243** is the assay method for water-extractable protein in NR medical gloves. It is not a sampling plan, a safety limit, or a labeling rule.

Fashion sheet almost never prints these numbers. Light and stretch cannot replace a lab extraction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: What aged sheet looks like</summary>

Ozone cracks run **perpendicular to stress** and usually cover a wide area. Flex cracks are also oriented but stay in the high-strain zone. UV cracking is non-oriented over the exposed face.

On the bench: sticky tack (chain scission), hard or brittle (extra crosslinks), chalky bloom (wax or antidegradant), oriented cracks (ozone or flex). Polish does not change ozone-crack shape.

</details>
