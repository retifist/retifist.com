# Sheet Edge Finishing Materials

> **Safety.** Bonding a tube or strip to commercial sheet usually means solvent rubber cement: fire and fume class. Read the **product** SDS. See [Sheet adhesives](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity) and [Sheet ventilation](/literature-reviews/sheet-ventilation-solvent-exposure).

## Executive summary

Edge finishing on bought sheet is a **material** choice first: NR tube or strip, or a rubberised textile tape stuck with solvent cement. The wrong family shows up as edge lift or a stiff rim that cracks in bend. This page names families and risks. It does not teach how to fold a curve.

Glue-family tree, [flash off](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity#flash-off), and [open time](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity#open-time): [Sheet adhesives](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity). Textile prep: [Sheet latex–textile bonding](/literature-reviews/sheet-latex-textile-bonding).

## Material families {#material-families}

| Family | Typical use | Risk cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NR tube / strip | Smooth finished rim | Match gauge to the sheet; cement [open time](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity#open-time) |
| Rubberised textile tape | High-stress or curved edge | Prep the cloth — [Sheet latex–textile bonding](/literature-reviews/sheet-latex-textile-bonding) |
| Woven cotton binding | Absorbent mechanical key | Thirsty face; still a solvent-cement job on sheet |
| Smooth filament tape | Slick face | [Polarity](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity#polarity) match required |

---

## Decision cues {#decision-cues}

```text
Edge geometry?
  straight high-stress → NR strip / tube family
  tight curve → the rim still has to stretch with the sheet
Surface?
  porous cotton → mechanical key if cement enters the weave
  slick nylon / filament → polarity match, not just more cement
Adhesive?
  solvent cement → [Sheet adhesives](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity)
```

A **lap** at the rim is still an overlap: peel starts at the free edge. Failed rims: [Sheet delamination](/literature-reviews/sheet-delamination-seam-failure).

## Deep dives (optional)

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Polarity and porous flow at tape edges</summary>

On a smooth tape face, polarity of the cement should match the face. On porous tape, the bond is mainly mechanical if adhesive enters the pores. Higher filler reduces flow into pores. Same rules as the adhesives review; the rim is just a narrow lap.
