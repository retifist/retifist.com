# Prevulcanized Consumer Latex

> **Safety.** Prevulcanization is a factory liquid-state cure with curatives. It is not “safer because no oven.” Ammonia, accelerators, and unknown bottle chemistry still matter. See [Allergy and Skin Contact](/literature-reviews/allergy-and-skin-contact).

## Executive summary

**Prevulcanized** latex is vulcanized **in the liquid** without waiting for moisture loss. A properly prevulcanized deposit can develop high tensile with **drying alone**. Industrial glove lines cite warm-air dry as sufficient. The handbook cites about 4,000–5,000 psi when particles integrate on drying.

If the liquid is **overcured** in the tank, the rubber is short and weak. Prevulcanized films peak at a **lower crosslink** than post-vulcanized latex films. Particle integration on drying is the limiter.

Do not assume every consumer bottle is prevulcanized NR. Raw concentrate is not a finished article without compounding. Styrene-acrylic theatrical dispersions are a different polymer family. Read [Decoding liquid latex bottle labels](/literature-reviews/liquid-decoding-bottle-labels) before you pick a finish branch.

---

## Dry-only vs oven-cure {#dry-vs-oven}

| Feedstock | Typical finish branch |
| --- | --- |
| Proper prevulc NR compound | Warm-air **dry** may suffice |
| Raw / under-compounded latex | Post-form **vulcanization** after dry |
| Unknown consumer bottle | Read the TDS, or treat as non-prevulc until proven |

“Hang to dry” is valid only when the liquid was actually prevulcanized NR with a controlled cure state. Plants use chloroform / swell QC.

<details>
<summary>What does prevulcanized mean?</summary>

**Prevulcanized** latex has already been partly crosslinked while still liquid. Curatives react in the tank before you dip or cast. **Post-vulcanized** film cures after the deposit dries. The two reach different modulus peaks. Overcooking prevulc stock in storage weakens the final rubber.

</details>

In the Practical Guide’s **casting** section, strength is always low for film from prevulcanized compound. Vanderbilt’s prevulc definition chapter says a proper prevulc can approach conventional properties. Those are two loci. Keep them labeled.

---

## Shelf life and clarifying {#shelf-life}

Remove unreacted sulfur and zinc oxide by centrifuge or decant so the liquid does not keep curing in storage. Add antioxidants **after** clarifying so they are not consumed in the heat step.

---

## Branch mix-ups {#do-not-mix}

- Prevulc dry-only logic on **raw concentrate**
- Medical glove prevulc TDS on a **theatrical acrylic bottle**

Name collision is not chemistry. See [Forming film from liquid latex](/literature-reviews/liquid-film-pathway).

## Deep dives (optional)

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Prevulcanization tank outline</summary>

Industrial prevulcanization heats compound to 70–80 °C (handbook example: about an hour at 79 °C), holds, cools, then clarifies. Ultra-accelerators allow atmospheric cure below 100 °C. Plant cards, not home tank recipes.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Modulus peak comparison</summary>

Postvulcanized NR latex films: tensile maximum near relaxed modulus about 0.8 MPa. Prevulcanized NR latex films: maximum at about 0.4–0.5 MPa (about 1–2×10⁻² mol dm⁻³ chemical crosslinks). High-crosslink drop in prevulcanizates is likely interference with particle integration on drying.

</details>
