# Liquid Reinforcement and Laminate Zones

Human page: https://retifist.com/literature-reviews/liquid-reinforcement-laminate-zones

## Executive summary

Liquid work builds strength into the film stack: multiple dips, NR inner with CR or XNBR outer, or textile combining before cut.[^3][^4] Inter-ply bond drops if the first layer is dried and leached; fresh wet gel maximizes adhesion; fatty soaps (potassium caprylate) hurt inter-ply adhesion.[^2] Low RH can impair second-dip lamination.[^5] Glue: [Liquid adhesives](/literature-reviews/liquid-adhesives-and-seam-integrity).

Fabric-lined gloves stretch less than unsupported gloves but stop tear propagation — analog, not a lining SOP.[^1]

## In-build laminates

**Multi-dip.** Dried/leached underlayer → weak ply; wet gel → next dip soon.[^2] Prevulcanized lab latex weaker than industrial prevulcanized in that account.[^2]

**NR inner + synthetic outer.** CR ozone/oil or XNBR oil on the outer face; NR strength inside.[^3] Blends of very different prevulcanization states weaken wet gel (mud-crack risk).[^3]

**Textile.** Porous mechanical key possible.[^7] Aqueous adhesive shrinks textiles.[^8] Plant combining: knife-over-roll, spray nip, heat-sensitive face dried first, doubling of pre-coated fabrics.[^4] See [Liquid latex–textile bonding](/literature-reviews/liquid-latex-textile-bonding).

## Zone map

| Zone | Liquid cue |
| --- | --- |
| Opening edge | Combine textile while wet if lining |
| Strap / harness root | Fabric laminate tear-stop analog |
| Hardware perimeter | Cu ageing[^6] |
| Whole-wall ply | Second dip on wet gel |

## Decision cues

```text
1. Tear-stop vs stretch management
2. Wet gel → next dip soon | Dried/leached → weak ply
3. Porous mechanical key | Aqueous shrink risk
```

<summary>Deep dive: Wet-gel window</summary>
