# Garment Selection QA for Buyers & Wearers

Human page: https://retifist.com/literature-reviews/garment-selection-qa-buyers-wearers

> **Not medical advice.** Pre-wear checks are **literacy**, not allergy testing or device certification.

## Executive summary

Before you wear or resell a latex garment, industrial dipped-goods specs offer a **borrowed vocabulary**—holes, powder, protein, odor, bloom, gauge variation—even though **fashion catsuits are not ASTM D3578 gloves** [^2]. NIOSH separates irritant, delayed chemical, and immediate protein reaction *patterns* for workplace NRL products [^3]. Use this when buying secondhand sheet goods, inspecting a custom commission, or deciding go/no-go on first wear. Pointer to [Allergy and Skin Contact](/literature-reviews/allergy-and-skin-contact) for allergy classes.

---

## Borrowed QC vocabulary (analogy, not certification)

ASTM **D3578** is the product standard for **dipped NR examination gloves** (holes, powder, protein, tensile) [^2]. ASTM **D5151** detects water-leak holes in **medical gloves** [^4]. Fashion sheet/garments sit **outside** that scope — transfer is analogy only.

Dipped-goods defects include entrapped air → pinholes, webbing, low wet gel, incomplete leach → worse aging/discoloration [^5].

---

## Pre-wear checklist (wearer literacy)

1. **Light + stretch** for pinholes and thin spots.
2. **Powder/talc** — medical powdered gloves banned as unreasonable risk [^6].
3. **Odor** — reodorants mask at 0.25–0.5 phr; root cause preferred [^7].
4. **Bloom vs crack** — [Aging, Storage and Care](/literature-reviews/aging-storage-and-care) [^8].
5. **Stop rule** — new rash/breathing symptoms → stop wear; see #03.

21 CFR **801.437** requires NRL caution on **medical devices** [^9].
