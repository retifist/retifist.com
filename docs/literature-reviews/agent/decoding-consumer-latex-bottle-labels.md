# Decoding Consumer Latex Bottle Labels: Concentrate Fields vs Marketing

Human page: https://retifist.com/literature-reviews/decoding-consumer-latex-bottle-labels

> **Label literacy, not medical advice.** Food-contact rubber tables and industrial concentrate specs do **not** certify fashion-garment wear clearance. See [Allergy and Skin Contact](/literature-reviews/allergy-and-skin-contact) for allergy classes.

## Executive summary

Consumer “liquid latex” bottles rarely print the same fields industrial buyers expect on **concentrate** specs. **Liquid latex work** shoppers need to decode ammonia band, total solids, prevulcanized vs raw, and polymer family—not marketing words like “body safe” or “FDA.” Industrial standards **ASTM D1076** and **ISO 2004** define ammonia-preserved NR **concentrate** categories; they **do not apply to compounded** hobby bottles [^2]. Use this when choosing a Path B bottle, comparing two brands, or when a seller cites food-contact or medical glove language out of scope.

---

## Label fields that actually mean something

ASTM **D1076** covers concentrated, ammonia-stabilized NR latex categories and **does not apply to compounded** concentrates [^2]. ISO **2004** specifies ammonia-preserved centrifuged/creamed concentrate types including HA, LA, and MA bands with Cu/Mn limits on total solids [^3].

**Prevulcanized latex** is vulcanized in the **liquid** state; a proper prevulc deposit can approach conventional post-dried properties; overcure yields short, weak rubber [^4]. Gloves from prevulcanized latex **need only warm-air drying** in the dipped-goods branch [^5].

Fresh latex coagulates within hours without preservation; ammonia is the primary preservative [^6].

## Marketing words that are not specs

| Label phrase | What it is **not** |
| --- | --- |
| “FDA approved” on a bottle | Usually food-contact ingredient tables (21 CFR **177.2600**) [^7] |
| “Medical grade” | Medical device rules — fashion garments typically outside scope |
| “Body safe / skin safe” | No universal standard in this literature pack |
| “100% latex” | May mean NR, synthetic, or acrylic — polymer family must be read |

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: HA / LA / MA ammonia bands</summary>

ISO **2004** types include HA, LA, and MA with Cu/Mn caps [^3]. Effective ammonia typically **>0.35%**; long storage often **0.6–1.0%** [^6]. Ask vendors for ammonia as NH₃ on wet latex [^12].

</details>

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Prevulcanized vs post-cure branch</summary>

Prevulcanized NR films peak tensile at lower modulus than postvulcanized films [^13]. Gloves from prevulcanized latex need only warm air drying [^5]. Post-form oven vulcanization is a separate branch [^14].

</details>
