# Printing & Surface Decoration on Latex Sheet

> **Safety.** Solvent inks and chlorination chemistry belong to industrial PPE contexts. This article defines **good** in industrial terms — not a print-shop tutorial.

## Executive summary

NR sheet stretches like an elastomer, not like cotton. Decoration must survive large strain without cracking or peeling. Industrial stretch-ink patents use **polyurethane/polyester elastomer vehicles** with pigment dispersions on **latex rubber** substrates.

Use this when judging whether an ink or transfer method matches NR elongation, and when [surface finish](#surface-prep-literacy) (gloss vs matte) is ignored in prep. Pair with [Surface Finish Literacy](/literature-reviews/surface-finish-literacy-gloss-matte-bloom) and [Pigment & Filler Decks](/literature-reviews/pigment-filler-decks-wearable-nr-film) for bulk color vs surface decoration.

---

## Why NR sheet is a hard print substrate {#hard-substrate}

Vulcanized film must stretch to at least **twice** its length under stress and recover. Rigid textile inks assume the wrong substrate class until proven otherwise.

<details>
<summary>What is a stretch-ink vehicle?</summary>

A **stretch-ink vehicle** is the binder system that carries pigment and must flex with the rubber underneath. Textile screen inks built for cotton do not automatically survive 200%+ elongation on latex sheet. Industrial patents specify elastomer vehicles (polyurethane, polyester) matched to latex rubber substrates.

</details>

---

## Stretch-ink architecture {#stretch-ink}

Stretchable ink patents target **latex rubber** substrates with high elongation without cracking (US10487228 family).

Ask vendors for elongation data on NR-like substrates — marketing photos are not a spec sheet.

---

## QC language {#qc-language}

ASTM D751 adhesion, D3389 abrasion, and D1876 T-peel provide **method classes** for coating and flexible bonds. They give vocabulary for comparing ink systems; they are not a pass/fail grade for your catsuit print.

| Standard | What it measures |
| --- | --- |
| ASTM D751 | Coated fabric adhesion |
| ASTM D3389 | Coated fabric abrasion |
| ASTM D1876 | T-peel flexible bonds |

See [Adhesives and Seam Integrity](/literature-reviews/adhesives-and-seam-integrity) for peel vocabulary.

## Surface prep literacy {#surface-prep-literacy}

Glazed former finish → shiny, vibrant colors; bisque → dull flat colors. Halogenation reduces tack permanently — see [Surface Finish Literacy](/literature-reviews/surface-finish-literacy-gloss-matte-bloom). Print-after-chlorination on fashion sheet needs its own prep branch; do not assume raw sheet face equals print-ready face.

---

## Sheet-based vs liquid latex work {#pathways}

**Sheet-based work:** decorate purchased face finish.

**Liquid latex work:** former or bed sets gloss before print; bulk color may be compounded — see [Pigment & Filler Decks](/literature-reviews/pigment-filler-decks-wearable-nr-film).
