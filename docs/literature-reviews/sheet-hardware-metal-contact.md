# Sheet Hardware & Metal Contact

## Executive summary

A snap or eyelet on cut sheet is a hole you punched and a metal face that sits on the rubber. Brass, copper, and mystery “antique” platings catalyze oxidative aging and leave stain rings that polish does not undo.

Inspect worn sheet for green or brown [halos](#inspection) and for a stiff ring around the fastener. A plastic washer can keep metal off the film. It does not cancel copper chemistry. Pair with the [Compatibility Matrix](/literature-reviews/compatibility-matrix-metals-oils-plastics) for the contact grid and [Sheet Aging, Storage and Care](/literature-reviews/sheet-aging-storage-and-care) for what aged film looks like.

---

## Why the metal matters {#why-metals}

Trace copper speeds up degradation of unsaturated rubber. In latex, brass and copper fittings cause serious deterioration of the rubber. Industrial films that will see copper get a **2 phr** antioxidant budget: one part for ordinary oxidation, one part held for copper.

<details>
<summary>What is phr?</summary>

**phr** is parts per hundred rubber: parts by weight of additive per 100 parts of dry rubber. 2 phr antioxidant is 2 grams per 100 grams of rubber.

</details>

You are not compounding the sheet in a factory. The takeaway is simpler: do not park brass on the film and hope polish will save it.

## What the hardware is {#taxonomy}

Selection and inspection only.

| Hardware | How it usually sits on sheet | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Press snap | Punched disk stack | Plated brass with an unknown core |
| Eyelet / grommet | Crimped barrel | Crevice plus sweat |
| D-ring / buckle | Line load on a metal face | Budget galvanized steel |
| Corset busk | Broad steel contact | Carbon-steel rust stain |

---

## Finish name vs base metal {#finish-substrate}

“Gunmetal,” “antique brass,” and “nickel finish” are shop labels. They do not prove the metal under the plate. Stainless-looking is not the same as verified 304 or 316. Ask the vendor for the base metal. Spot-test a scrap of the same sheet before you commit a whole waistband.

---

## Avoid / conditional / OK {#avoid-ok}

| Metal / finish | Direct contact with sheet | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copper, brass, bronze | **Avoid** | Change the hardware. Polish is cosmetic. |
| Galvanized (wet or sweaty) | **Avoid** | Zinc-coated steel plus sweat is the wet-contact problem |
| Verified 304 / 316 stainless | **Conditional** | Clean stainless is acceptable plant metal. Sweat in the eyelet crevice is still an open condition. |
| Plastic / nylon washer | **Conditional** | Physical barrier only. Copper chemistry still applies if metal touches rubber. |
| Unknown plated snap | **Avoid** until tested | Run a scrap panel first |

---

## Inspection cues {#inspection}

- Brown or green halo around the fastener, like the stain on a copper pipe
- Stiff ring in the sheet around an eyelet
- Crack starting at the punched hole when you stretch the panel
- That patch ages faster than the sheet next to it (bend both and compare)

If the halo grows, replace the hardware. Polishing the stain does not stop the chemistry.
