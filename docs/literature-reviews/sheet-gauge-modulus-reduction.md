# Gauge, Modulus, and Reduction on Bought Sheet

## Executive summary

On bought [calendered](#what-calendered) sheet, fit is three knobs. [Gauge](#gauge) is how thick the roll is. [Modulus](#modulus) is how stiff that rubber already is at a given stretch. [Reduction](#reduction) is how much smaller you cut the pattern than the body.

Thinner, softer, and smaller-cut are different moves. Use this when you are picking a millimetre from a catalog, or when two rolls of the same printed thickness feel different in the hand.

---

## Gauge: the millimetre on the roll {#gauge}

**Gauge** is thickness, usually in millimetres. Retail garment-sheet menus commonly run about **0.25–1.05 mm**. That is a shopping list.

ISO 3302-1 includes calendered rubber sheet in its dimensional-tolerance scope. It is QC language for how close the measured thickness sits to the label. It is a tolerance framework for solid rubber, not a garment construction chapter.

How vendors write those menus: [Calendered latex sheet](/literature-reviews/sheet-calendered-sheet).

<details id="what-calendered">
<summary>What does calendered mean?</summary>

**Calendered** sheet is rubber pressed to thickness between heavy heated rollers. Fashion roll goods arrive already a film. You cut and glue what the mill sold.

</details>

## Modulus: stiffness the mill already set {#modulus}

**Modulus** is how much force the rubber takes to reach a given stretch. Handbooks quote it as MPa at 300% or as psi at 500%. Higher modulus feels boardier at the same thickness.

You do not mix this on the bench. The mill compounded the roll. Two blacks with the same printed thickness can still disagree in the hand if one carries more filler. That inherited-stiffness story is in [Colored sheet: pigment and filler effects](/literature-reviews/sheet-pigment-filler-decks).

Thickness still changes feel when the compound is the same. A thicker piece of the same rubber is harder to bend. On the body it asks for more force to reach the same stretch. That is geometry, not a new modulus number.

Elastomeric film is expected to stretch to at least twice its length and recover. Fit has to leave room inside that.

<details>
<summary>What is modulus?</summary>

**Modulus** here is stiffness at a stated stretch, in MPa or psi. It is not thickness. A soft thin sheet and a stiff thin sheet can share a gauge and wear nothing alike.

</details>

---

## Reduction: how small you cut the pattern {#reduction}

**Reduction** is pattern geometry. You draw the flat pieces smaller than the body measurements so the finished garment has to stretch to close. Pattern makers also call this negative ease.

---

## Decision cues {#decision-cues}

**Choose a catalog millimetre** when you want a different wall thickness. Read the vendor’s own spec.

**Treat same-gauge rolls as different compounds** when they feel different. Bagging after wear often traces to set in the mill compound, or to aging, not to a wrong stamp on the roll. See [Sheet aging, storage, and care](/literature-reviews/sheet-aging-storage-and-care).

**Change the pattern outline** when you want more or less negative ease.
