# Liquid Latex Painting vs Compounding Pigment

> **Safety.** Read TDS/SDS. Food-contact colorants ≠ wear clearance.

## Executive summary

**Compounding pigment** tints NR before film formation. **Surface paint** is a separate layer — often a **different polymer**. Consumer “latex paint” bottles need TDS polymer ID.

Use this when decoding product names, when someone proposes brush-on color for stretchy film, or alongside [Pigment & Filler Decks](/literature-reviews/pigment-filler-decks-wearable-nr-film) and [Decoding Consumer Latex Bottle Labels](/literature-reviews/decoding-consumer-latex-bottle-labels).

---

## Three classes {#three-classes}

| Class | Pathway |
| --- | --- |
| Compound pigment (dispersed in latex) | Liquid latex work |
| NR surface coat / prevulcanized deposit | Decoration branch |
| Acrylic or wrong polymer | Rejected for NR compound |

<details>
<summary>Why does polymer family matter?</summary>

**Natural rubber (NR)** film stretches and recovers through a vulcanized elastomer network. **Styrene-acrylic** or theatrical “latex paint” may dry into a rigid film that cracks on stretch. The word “latex” on a bottle does not guarantee NR — read the TDS polymer line.

</details>

---

## Compound prep {#compound-prep}

Dispersion **<5 µm** before adding to latex; emulsify oil-phase colorants first. Undispersed powder shocks the colloid; poor oil emulsion leaves spots on the film.

See [Pigment & Filler Decks](/literature-reviews/pigment-filler-decks-wearable-nr-film#identity-buckets) for identity buckets.
