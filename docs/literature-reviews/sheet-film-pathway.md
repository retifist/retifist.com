# Buying Formed Latex Sheet

> **Safety.** Natural-rubber protein and accelerator chemistry can matter for skin contact. That is literacy, not a wear certificate. See [Allergy and Skin Contact](/literature-reviews/allergy-and-skin-contact).

## Executive summary

This pathway starts with a roll you already bought. The mill formed the film. You cut it, glue it, and finish it.

Use this when you are shopping catalog sheet or reading a thickness and finish menu. For how the sheet is specified, see [Calendered latex sheet](/literature-reviews/sheet-calendered-sheet). For seams after you have film, see [Sheet adhesives and seam integrity](/literature-reviews/sheet-adhesives-and-seam-integrity).

## What you are buying {#what-you-buy}

Commercial latex sheet is sold as industrial or catalog goods: thickness menus, finish options, and cure-state options. Fashion suppliers usually mean **calendered** roll goods.

<details>
<summary>What does calendered mean?</summary>

**Calendered** sheet is rubber that was squeezed between heavy, heated rollers to an even thickness with smooth faces. You receive a formed film.

</details>

Shop culture around bought sheet is usually solvent rubber cement as a contact bond. That is a pathway label, not a handbook procedure. The glue article covers how contact bonds actually work.

Color, stretch, and shine do not change what you bought. A red roll and a black roll are still catalog sheet.

---

## How thickness is specified {#thickness}

Catalogs quote thickness in millimetres. ISO 3302-1 is a dimensional-tolerance framework that includes calendered rubber sheet. It is a tolerance class for solid rubber, not a mill recipe and not a garment construction chapter.

Vendor tables that interpret those ST classes for thin sheet are guidance, not the standard text. One published example: 0–1 mm at ST1 as ±0.15 mm. Read your supplier’s own spec.

Manufacturer category pages describe calendered natural-rubber sheeting from about 300 µm, with smooth or cloth finish and cure-state options. Retail garment-sheet menus often run about 0.25–1.05 mm, sometimes with a polishable upper face and a roughened underside. Those are shopping cues. Categorize them. Do not treat a catalog chart as an audited fashion mill process.

## After you have the sheet {#after-the-roll}

Once the film exists, heat, light, ozone, oils, and metals still age it. See [Sheet aging, storage, and care](/literature-reviews/sheet-aging-storage-and-care).

Tensile and tear test methods (ASTM D412, ASTM D624) can describe vulcanized rubber. They are method classes. They are not a shop QC card for a catsuit.

Protein versus accelerator versus irritant classes stay literacy. See [Allergy and Skin Contact](/literature-reviews/allergy-and-skin-contact).

---

## When this pathway fits {#when}

You need a usable film now. You accept the catalog thickness and finish as given. Repeatability is “the mill already made the sheet.”

If you are forming thickness or shape from a bottle, that is a different pathway: [Forming film from liquid latex](/literature-reviews/liquid-film-pathway).
