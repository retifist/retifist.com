# Painting vs Compounding Pigment

> **Safety.** Read the TDS and SDS for ammonia, solvents, and pigments. A food-contact colorant list is not a wear clearance.

## Executive summary

**Compounding pigment** tints the latex before the film forms. **Surface paint** is a second layer on cured rubber, and that layer is often a different polymer. A bottle labeled “liquid latex” or “latex paint” is not automatically compound-grade NR pigment. Read the polymer line on the TDS.

Use this when you are choosing a tint method or decoding a consumer bottle. How to prepare a real compound tint lives in [Pigment and Filler Compounding](/literature-reviews/liquid-pigment-filler-decks). How to read a bottle lives in [Decoding Consumer Latex Bottle Labels](/literature-reviews/liquid-decoding-bottle-labels).

## Three classes {#three-classes}

| Class | What it is | Where it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compound pigment | Pigment dispersed in the latex before you cast or dip | The industrial color path for film that is colored through its thickness |
| NR surface coat | A separate aqueous NR or [prevulcanized](#what-prevulc) deposit on an already-formed film | Decoration, not compounding |
| Wrong polymer | Styrene-acrylic, vinyl-acetate, or hardware-store “latex paint” | Rejected as an NR compound tint |

Raw latex film is not a saleable article until it is compounded. Class-3 dyes and pigments are optional modifiers in that package, next to sulfur and zinc oxide. That is class one.

<details id="what-prevulc">
<summary>What does prevulcanized mean here?</summary>

**Prevulcanized** (prevulc) NR is latex that was already crosslinked in the liquid state. A prevulc deposit can dry into a rubbery coat. That is still a *layer on top of* a film, with its own dry-only rules. It is not the same as mixing pigment into the batch that *becomes* the film. See [Prevulcanized Consumer Latex](/literature-reviews/liquid-prevulcanized-consumer-latex).

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why does the word “latex” on a bottle lie?</summary>

Hardware-store **latex paint** is usually a styrene-acrylic or vinyl-acetate binder: a water-thinnable coating, not Hevea rubber. Industrial latex-paint history in the handbooks is that binder family. The front label is marketing. The TDS polymer line is the chemistry.

</details>

## Compound tint {#compound-prep}

Water-insoluble solids go in as a dispersion under **5 µm** before they meet the latex. Immiscible oils and oil-carried colorants go in as an oil-in-water emulsion first. Skip that prep and you get a shocked compound or oil spots on the film.

Shaking craft pigment into a bottle is not that prep. Full buckets, Hegman checks, and slurry rules are in [Pigment and Filler Compounding](/literature-reviews/liquid-pigment-filler-decks#identity-buckets).

---

## Surface coats and stretch {#surface-stretch}

A surface coat has to elongate with the NR underneath. A rigid acrylic film on a stretchy NR film is two different networks stacked together. Elastomer-ink architecture is the documented way to decorate a stretching face. A prevulc NR deposit is at least the same polymer family; it is still a coat, not a through-color compound.

---

## Decision cues {#decision-cues}

**Tint the batch** when you want color through the thickness and you can run dispersion or emulsion prep.

**Use a prevulc or NR-compatible coat** when you are decorating a film that already exists and you can live with a separate layer.

**Read the TDS polymer** when the bottle says “latex.” If it is styrene-acrylic or vinyl-acetate, it is class three.

## Deep dives (optional)

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Dispersion vs emulsion failure</summary>

Dispersion: Hegman grind check; remix before you weigh. Emulsion: drop test on water; homogenize at or below 38°C; watch the casein swell window. Same prep rules as the compounding review; the failure modes are oil spots and grit, not “weak color.”

</details>

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Name collision</summary>

Prevulc NR and styrene-acrylic construction dispersions can share look-alike brand stems and the word latex. One vulcanized in the liquid state and can make a rubbery deposit. The other is a paint binder. Do not substitute by the name on the cap.

</details>
