# Zipper Closure Material Compatibility

> **Safety.** Solvent cement culture on sheet-based work — ventilate. Cleaners at the tape edge can attack adhesive — see [Compatibility Matrix](/literature-reviews/compatibility-matrix-metals-oils-plastics).

## Executive summary

This article is **compatibility only** — tape, coil, and stop materials — not how to install a zipper. Nylon coil is folk-default because continuous filament nylon presents a smooth face with **poor mechanical bonding** to rubber without a prep bridge. Aqueous rubberise or polarity prep is an **industrial strategy**. Latex adhesives **shrink fabrics** — relevant to woven tape.

Do not cite ASTM D2054 as an NR-sheet crocking method — scope is **zipper tape textile** only. Pair with [Latex Textile Bonding](/literature-reviews/latex-textile-bonding), [Adhesives and Seam Integrity](/literature-reviews/adhesives-and-seam-integrity), and [Hardware & Metal Contact](/literature-reviews/hardware-metal-contact-snaps-eyelets) for metal coil.

## Nylon tape and coil {#nylon}

Polyamide and polyester continuous filaments lack cotton-like fiber ends for mechanical embed. On non-porous faces, latex polarity should match surfaces. Porous cotton tape branch differs — see textile bonding article.

<details>
<summary>What is a prep bridge?</summary>

A **prep bridge** is a surface treatment — abrasion, primer, or rubberise coat — that gives slick filament tape something for latex cement to grip. Smooth nylon and polyester do not embed mechanically the way cotton weave does.

</details>

## Decision cues {#decision-cues}

```text
Tape fiber?
  cotton → mechanical key easier; shrink if aqueous
  nylon/PET → prep bridge required (industrial)
Adhesive family?
  sheet-based solvent culture → adhesives article
  aqueous on tape → shrink check
Coil metal?
  yes → compatibility matrix + hardware article
```

## Deep dives (optional)

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Nylon filament mechanical bond limits</summary>

Rayon, nylon, polyester: smooth waxlike surfaces — poor mechanical bonding unless abraded or chemically bridged. Tire-cord abrasion plus latex–casein sequence is industrial — partial analog for apparel tape.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: ASTM D2054 scope boundary</summary>

ASTM D2054 covers colorfastness of **zipper tapes** to crocking — textile QC, not NR sheet bond strength.

</details>
