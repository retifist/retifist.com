# Heat-Sensitized Gelation

> **Safety.** Ammonium nitrate sensitizer is added just before use. Avoid entraining air. Heat plus a closed mould means burn and scald risk. No copper or brass in latex contact.

## Executive summary

**Heat-sensitized** compounds gel when a **heated mould** triggers a sensitizer package. Industrial examples include ammonium nitrate, zinc-ammine, and polyvinyl methyl ether. Use this for **closed-cavity** hollow shells.

**Slush moulding:** fill the mould, dwell, pour out excess. Thickness follows compound stability and geometry. **Rotational moulding:** meter compound, multi-axis rotation while heating. More uniform walls.

A handbook **Kaysam** cycle cites about 4 minutes at 82 °C to gel, then cool, leach, supported dry, and warm-air cure. **Dry completely before vulcanizing.** Moisture causes blisters.

Read this after [Former dip, mould cast, and flat spread](/literature-reviews/liquid-former-dip-vs-mould-cast-vs-flat-spread) routes you to a closed metal mould. Coagulant on a former is a different trigger: [Coagulant dipping](/literature-reviews/liquid-coagulant-dipping-wearable-thickness).

## Heat-gel vs coagulant-gel {#heat-vs-coagulant}

| Mechanism | Trigger | Typical hardware |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coagulant | Coacervant on a **former** | Dip tank, leach |
| Heat-sensitized | **Mould temperature** plus sensitizer in the compound | Closed metal mould, rotation |

Plaster moulds gel by **water absorption plus calcium diffusion**, not a heat sensitizer. Keep plaster cards and Kaysam rotation cards apart.

---

## Kaysam industrial cycle {#kaysam-cycle}

Fill a closed mould. Biaxial rotation. Heat to gel (about 4 minutes at 82 °C is often cited). Cool the mould. Demold the wet gel. Leach in running water. Dry supported at elevated temperature. Cure in warm air.

Sensitizer (20% ammonium nitrate solution) is added **just before use**.

---

## Slush on metal {#slush-metal}

Heat-sensitized natural rubber for slush moulding in metal moulds preheated 90–100 °C. Gel 2–5 minutes depending on deposit. Handbook formulations use zinc-ammine (ammonium acetate) or PVME sensitizer packages. Industrial examples, not craft recipes.

---

## Rotational casting {#rotational}

Metered compound. Rotation while heating. Magnesium moulds are preferred over aluminum for alkaline corrosion resistance in balloon-class processes.

---

## Decision cues {#decision-cues}

```text
Closed hollow cavity + metal mould? → heat-sensitized slush or rotational
Need wall uniformity? → prefer rotational (if equipment exists)
Plaster one-off? → absorption gel branch (forming article), not metal slush
Have supported dry + leach? → if no, do not scale gel time alone
```

## Deep dives (optional)

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Kaysam full timing</summary>

Handbook cycle: biaxial rotation; about 4 minutes at 82 °C to gel; cool 2–5 minutes at 15 °C; leach 15–27 °C; dry 82 °C at least 3 hours, supported; cure 104 °C for 20–60 minutes (thickness-dependent). Sensitizer mixed just before use.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Deep dive: Slush formulation classes</summary>

Industrial NR slush formulations use zinc-ammine (ammonium acetate) or PVME sensitizer packages in metal moulds preheated 90–100 °C. Nonionic ethoxylates stabilize zinc oxide / ammonia systems at ambient in the handbook’s section.

</details>
