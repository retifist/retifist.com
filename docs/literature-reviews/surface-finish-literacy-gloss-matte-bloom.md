# Surface Finish Literacy: Gloss, Matte, Bloom & Chlorination Cues

> **Safety.** Halogenation uses corrosive oxidizers — vocabulary only, not a home tutorial.

## Executive summary

**Gloss** and **matte** often come from **how the film was formed**, not from a post-cure polish alone. **Tack** may be cut temporarily (talc) or permanently (halogenation). **Bloom** is a migrating layer — not necessarily dirt.

Use this when reading sheet faces, when [storage-related surface change](/literature-reviews/aging-storage-and-care) appears, or before [printing](/literature-reviews/printing-surface-decoration-sheet) on a face whose finish you did not control.

## Finish vocabulary map {#finish-map}

| Cue | What it usually means |
| --- | --- |
| Gloss / vibrant color | Glazed former (liquid latex work) |
| Matte / flat color | Bisque former (liquid latex work) |
| Permanent de-tack | Halogenation (liquid latex post-cure) |
| Powder after storage | Wax bloom |
| Dual-face fashion sheet | Vendor-dependent; ask which face is which |

---

## Liquid latex work — former finish {#former-finish}

<details>
<summary>What is a glazed vs bisque former?</summary>

A **glazed former** has a smooth, polished surface — dipped or cast film against it dries shiny with vibrant color. A **bisque** (or sandblasted) former has a micro-rough face — film dries dull and matte. The finish is baked in during formation, not applied like furniture polish afterward.

</details>

---

## Halogenation {#halogenation}

Halogenation (chlorination) permanently reduces surface tack. Handbook example parameters: ~0.15% Cl, 4.5–7.5 min tumble; neutralize, rinse, dry 40–46°C; no copper or brass contact. Chlorinated faces change how [inks and adhesives](/literature-reviews/adhesives-and-seam-integrity) behave.

---

## Bloom {#bloom}

<details>
<summary>What is wax bloom?</summary>

**Wax bloom** is antidegradant wax migrating to the rubber surface to form a temporary ozone barrier. Microcrystalline wax at 0.5–1.0 phr blooms over hours; flexing and stretching crack the bloom layer and reduce protection. Bloom looks like chalky powder — it is chemistry, not necessarily dirt.

</details>

See [Antidegradants in DIY Compounds](/literature-reviews/antidegradants-diy-compounds) and [Aging, Storage and Care](/literature-reviews/aging-storage-and-care).

---

## Sheet-based work note {#sheet-based}

Calendered sheet may ship with distinct face and back finishes from the mill. Vendor menus describe gloss/matte options — read the catalog rather than guessing which face prints best.
