# Chlorination for Tack Control & Skin Contact

> **Safety.** Chlorine, hypochlorite, and bromine are oxidizing and corrosive. Neutralize before final rinses; process develops acidity. **Halogenation reduces tack; it is not a substitute for allergy literacy** — see [Allergy and Skin Contact](/literature-reviews/allergy-and-skin-contact). No copper or brass contact.

## Executive summary

Cured liquid-latex film is often **tacky**. **Talc** is temporary industrial dusting. **Permanent de-tack** in dipped-goods plants uses aqueous **halogenation** — chlorine water, sodium hypochlorite, or bromine water — typically in a tumbler, then neutralize, rinse, dry **40–46°C**.

Handbook example: treatment **4.5–7.5 min** at room temperature with **~0.15% chlorine** (from saturated stock). **Higher sulfur** cured goods need **less** chlorine time than low-sulfur films. Pair with [Surface Finish Literacy](/literature-reviews/surface-finish-literacy-gloss-matte-bloom) for gloss/matte context; chlorination is a liquid-latex post-cure finish, not a sheet-based default.

## Solution choice {#solution-choice}

| Solution | Tack reduction | Discoloration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chlorine water | ≈ bromine | Least among three |
| Bromine water | Strong | More than Cl |
| Hypochlorite (e.g. household bleach) | Less efficient | Similar discoloration to Br |

Hypochlorite is easier to source than cylinder chlorine; revalidate time and concentration on **your** film sulfur level.

---

## Process discipline {#process-discipline}

After treatment: **neutralize** (~1% sodium thiosulfate or ammonia); **three water rinses** with tumbling; **dry warm-air tumbler 40–46°C**. Fresh chlorine solution per batch. Skipping neutralize or rinse leaves acidic residues — skin-contact and aging risk.

<details>
<summary>What does halogenation do to the surface?</summary>

**Halogenation** (chlorination or bromination) chemically modifies the rubber surface to cut tack permanently. It is not the same as embossing for grip — embossing alone is insufficient when wet grip is needed on dipped goods. Halogenation also does **not** remove natural-rubber protein — allergy classes remain in force.

</details>

---

## Sheet-based work note {#sheet-based}

Bought calendered sheet may arrive talc-dusted or polished — chlorination lore is **liquid-latex finish** lineage. Do not assume a sheet roll needs glove-line chlorination.
